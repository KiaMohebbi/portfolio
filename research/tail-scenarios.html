Tail model and scenario generator

The tail shape and the scenario generator behind the CVaR objective were chosen, audited, rebased onto the certified predictive covariance and finally simplified to a single-regime Student-t.
Published

July 22, 2026

Regime drift 2026-07-18 adopted “D1 (beta-scaled market-factor drift) ADOPTED” (specification changelog); later “R-M1 inherits the D1 location” (specification, scenario generation) and the simplification gave up “D1 skew placement” (specification, simplification section)
Scenario and CVaR layer audit 2026-07-22 rejected “M4 mixture does NOT earn the scenario job” (scenario audit report); the mixture stayed the production generator until the simplification (specification, rebase section)
Scenario-layer rebase 2026-07-22 adopted “the rebase RESOLVES the ERA-2 many-but-mild breach pattern” (rebase study report)
Scenario generator simplification 2026-07-22 adopted “R-M1 NON-INFERIOR on every pre-registered metric -> DEPLOY” (simplification study report)

This program asked what distribution the CVaR objective should draw its scenarios from: how fat the tails should be, whether a regime mixture or an explicit copula should shape them, and how expected return should be split across regimes inside the scenario generator. The early tests adopted a regime mixture with within-regime Student-t tails and a mean-preserving market-factor drift, and found that per-regime degrees of freedom and copulas did not earn a place. A later audit found that the mixture only tied a single-regime Student-t on weekly tail mass, and that the generator was still drawing from a covariance pool the predictive path had retired; once the wiring was corrected, the mixture’s remaining multi-period edge reversed. The program ended with the scenario generator simplified to a single-regime iterated Student-t on the certified predictive covariance, with the rebased mixture kept as its tested rollback; no generator tested covered the crash-arrival weeks.

Tail-risk model test: is CVaR calibrated, and does a copula help? (regime mixture plus within-regime fat tails; copulas do not earn a keep)

Logged 2026-07-17.

Eight scenario distributions scored on tail calibration at the monthly decision horizon. Every model’s CVaR was optimistic; the old single-covariance Student-t under-stated the tail worst in crisis; regime mixture and within-regime fat tails together fixed it; per-regime degrees of freedom and copulas added nothing decision-grade.

Question

Every calibration result up to this point was a second-moment test: realized versus predicted volatility. The optimizer’s objective is CVaR, a tail quantity, and a model can be perfectly volatility-calibrated and badly tail-calibrated. So: are VaR and CVaR breaches occurring at the nominal rate, does realized loss beyond VaR equal predicted CVaR, and does an explicit tail-dependent copula, or anything else, improve it?

The handoff from the state-count test framed the prior: fat tails live almost entirely in the crisis state, so a single global Student-t on one blended covariance is mis-specified in both directions, too fat for calm and too thin for crisis.

Design (fixed before looking at results)

Frozen. Weekly panel, the two-state model on the incumbent signal, soft-pooled shrinkage covariances, embargo, the walk-forward harness, and the single-use holdout, never entered. The only thing that varies across models is the scenario distribution.

Candidates. Single-covariance Gaussian (floor); single-covariance Student-t (the old production); filtered historical simulation (nonparametric benchmark); regime mixture with Gaussian within regime; mixture with Student-t marginals and one global degrees of freedom; mixture with per-regime degrees of freedom; mixture with a Student-t copula (symmetric tail dependence); mixture with a Clayton copula (asymmetric lower-tail dependence).

Three design decisions. First, marginals and copula were separated: the mixture models use a Gaussian copula with Student-t marginals, and the copula variants change only the copula, so the step between them isolates the copula’s contribution beyond fat marginals and the mixture. Second, multi-period Markov scenarios: never square-root-scale a weekly covariance to the monthly horizon; simulate forward week by week from the filtered regime belief, letting the chain transition, and compound, so scenarios where the regime shifts mid-horizon arise naturally. Third, two clocks: per-regime covariances, means, and degrees of freedom on the slow clock; only the filtered belief updates on the fast one.

Scoring. Kupiec unconditional coverage, Christoffersen independence and conditional coverage (do breaches cluster?), the ES-to-CVaR ratio as the headline for a CVaR objective, pinball loss, and an empirical-versus-simulated lower-tail dependence diagnostic for key pairs (gold-equity, dollar-emerging, credit-equity, developed-domestic). Every metric split calm versus crisis by the model’s own filtered probability.

Power, reported before any verdict. Roughly one hundred fifty monthly out-of-sample observations give only a handful of expected breaches at the primary level, and the higher confidence level cannot be reliably backtested at this sample. The verdict rests on the primary level; the higher one is directional only.

Outcome

Result: every model’s CVaR is optimistic, and the fix is regime conditioning plus within-regime fat tails, not a copula.

  • Realized loss beyond VaR exceeded predicted CVaR for all eight models on the primary read, and every model breached slightly more than nominal. Kupiec never rejected, exactly as the power analysis warned it could not. The verdict rests on the one-signed ES ratio and the regime split, not on breach counts.
  • The old production single-covariance Student-t under-states the tail, worst in crisis windows and at the higher confidence level. Because the optimizer maximizes return per unit of CVaR, an optimistic CVaR means it was quietly running more tail risk than it believed.
  • The mixture helps only with within-regime fat tails. The mixture with Gaussian marginals left the crisis tail badly optimistic, worse than production. Between-regime kurtosis handles the body; the crisis tail needs fat marginals inside the regime. The two are complements, not substitutes.
  • Per-regime degrees of freedom do not help at the decision level, and the per-window asset-level estimate is noisy. The Student-t copula adds essentially nothing over the mixture’s own tail dependence. The Clayton copula is rejected.

Decision

Production keeps a Gaussian copula times within-regime Student-t marginals with a single global degrees of freedom, the parsimonious form, on top of the regime mixture. The state-count handoff was resolved: per-regime fatness is indicated in-distribution but does not clear the decision-level bar out of sample.

The residual, shared under-reservation that survived the fix led to the conservatism multiplier decision, frozen before the holdout was opened: a uniform multiplier applied to scenario losses, never to the covariance (the body is already over-predicted, so the mismatch is shape, not scale); uniform because scaling every scenario loss by a constant leaves the STARR argmax unchanged and only raises reported risk. The shipped default was the raw model output, with a recommended buffer documented and pre-registered for activation at the sizing step; a regime-specific multiplier and a large uniform one were rejected as canonizing the noisiest cells. The single-use holdout later found the under-reservation did not reproduce, which vindicated shipping the raw number.

Where it led

  • The weekly slice study reached the same “fat margins, not vol dynamics” conclusion from a different angle, under a pre-registered protocol on a sealed holdout, and promoted Student-t innovations to the predictive standard for the whole risk model.
  • The fat Student-t margins are the load-bearing survivor of this test. The mixture half was retired with the generator in the scenario simplification.

Regime drift: how to split expected return across regimes inside the scenario generator (adopt D1)

Logged 2026-07-18.

A pre-registered ladder of drift methods for the regime-mixture scenario generator, constrained so the stationary mixture reproduces the strategic mu exactly and drift can only inject skew. The one-parameter beta-scaled market drift was adopted on the tie-breakers; the richer methods overfit.

Question

One decision, fixed before the engine merge: how should the regime drift vector, the per-regime split of expected return inside the regime-mixture scenario generator, be applied, and does it improve tail calibration at all? Everything else was frozen: the weekly spliced panel, the two-state model on the incumbent signal, filtered probabilities, the soft-pooled per-regime covariances, the per-regime degrees of freedom, the strategic consensus mean, and the holdout.

Pre-registration (the principle that makes drift safe)

The drift is applied only inside the scenario generator, split per regime, and constrained so that the stationary mixture reproduces the strategic mean exactly, per asset, as a hard constraint. Under the stationary start the state marginal is invariant under the transition matrix, so every simulated week has the strategic mean for any drift. Drift therefore moves no expected return; it injects only negative skew (crisis draws down, calm draws up, offsetting in the mean). The optimizer’s numerator stays strategic and no return timing leaks into weights. This is what makes regime drift safe where regime-conditional allocation means were not.

Corollary, the null. Drift can help the tail only if realized returns are asymmetric. Zero drift already gives symmetric fat tails via crisis widening and the within-regime Student-t. If crises are merely wider but not skewed, zero drift wins.

The ladder. Zero drift (the null); a one-parameter market-factor drift, assets inheriting via the frozen loadings so safe havens get the correct sign free through their negative market beta; a full observable-factor drift; a per-asset historical difference (the kitchen sink, expected to overfit); and the per-asset version shrunk toward the factor version.

Design. A phase-zero asymmetry diagnostic first (per-regime skewness, excess kurtosis, left-versus-right tail magnitude, and the raw crisis-minus-calm means with their standard errors). The mean-preservation identity asserted every method, every window, plus a stronger simulated check that the scenario mean equals the strategic mean for a nontrivial drift. A walk-forward master table scoring left and right tails separately on equal-weight and risk-parity probes, with skew gap and body bias. Estimation stability across windows. Synthetic positive (known negative crisis drift must be recovered) and negative (wider but symmetric must not produce manufactured skew) controls.

Outcome

Result: adopt D1, the beta-scaled market-factor drift, on the tie-breakers, not on an ES gain.

  • The asymmetry is real: the market factor and credit are left-skewed in crisis with crisis excess kurtosis, not merely wider. But it is small and factor-level; the tail magnitude ratio is modest and the raw per-asset crisis-minus-calm means are individually insignificant, the classic setup where a factor-structured estimate beats a per-asset one.
  • D1 is the best left-tail point estimate on both geometries, but the improvement over zero drift sits inside the sampling band, so by the pre-registered rule it is not a decision-grade calibration win and was not selected on ES alone.
  • The tie-breakers decide it: D1 halves the simulated-versus-realized skew gap zero drift leaves, does not break the right tail, does not touch the body (drift is mean-only with the covariance frozen), has the cleanest economic signs, and is the most stable.
  • The richer methods do not earn their parameters: no ES gain, worse skew match, several economic sign violations, and roughly twice the window-to-window instability. The observable-factor version even flips the sign of credit drift through rates-factor bleed.
  • Both synthetic controls passed. A mild right-tail coverage rejection is shared by every method, independent of drift, and was logged as out of scope.

Decision

D1 was adopted for the generator, with zero drift recorded as a defensible fallback. At the engine merge it was re-validated on the production hybrid covariance (it had been tested on the shrinkage configuration) and transferred directionally, with the finding that the hybrid’s production tail is slightly conservative where shrinkage’s was optimistic.

Where it led

  • The scenario rebase changed the covariance base the mixture draws from and left D1 unchanged.
  • When the generator was simplified to a single regime, the mean-preserving split degenerated to its mixture mean: the location D1 defines was retained as the scenario mean, and the mixture’s skew content left with the mixture, measured to have bought no metric win once the mixture was unhandicapped.

Scenario and CVaR layer audit: does the regime mixture earn the scenario job, and is crash arrival covered? (mixture ties the single-regime null; arrival beyond every cloud; a mis-wiring discovered)

Logged 2026-07-22.

A pre-registered audit of the tail machinery above the frozen certified predictive: does the regime-mixture scenario cloud place honest mass where realized weeks land, especially at crash arrival, and does scenario CVaR predict realized tail losses? The mixture did not beat the single-regime Student-t; no variant, including an explicit jump overlay, covers the arrival weeks; and the audit found the generator still drawing from the retired covariance pool.

Question

The performance profile established that the certified predictive structurally cannot absorb crash arrival: the V3 dial is a persistence bet that lags arrivals by construction. Tail protection against discontinuity, if it exists anywhere in the pipeline, lives in the regime-mixture scenario generator and the CVaR objective. This study tested whether it actually exists there. Two questions, registered verbatim: at each historical week, does the scenario cloud place honest probability mass at or beyond where the realized return landed, especially in the arrival weeks the covariance missed (Q1); and does the CVaR computed from the scenario set predict realized tail losses (Q2)?

Pre-registration (frozen and hashed before any scoring)

Frozen throughout. The deployed predictive (certified covariance times the frozen dial, expanding causal degrees of freedom), reconciled to the profile’s arrays. Nothing in the covariance was retuned; every treatment variable lives in the scenario layer. The regime machinery auditioned here as the uncondemned-but-untested survivor in the one role it kept after the window study: tail-mass placement. The predictive-path verdicts did not transfer automatically either way.

Variants, draw-count matched, seeded. The production mixture generator as configured (stationary seeding with the D1 drift), the incumbent; a single-regime Student-t drawn from the frozen one-step predictive and iterated over the horizon, the audition’s null and the bar the mixture had to beat; the mixture with filtered time-varying seeding (the weight-timing axis, with a recorded adaptation note: the prereg text asked for a flattened variant, but production was already stationary-seeded, so the registered intent required the filtered counterpart, recorded before scoring, not post hoc); and the single-regime Student-t plus a pre-registered jump overlay calibrated only on pre-2010 crash frequency, testing whether an explicit arrival mechanism is what is missing. Each scored with production drift and with zero drift as a sensitivity row. Probe portfolios recomputed weekly; the production allocator excluded as circular.

Controls, then a governed amendment. The battery’s controls, a known-good synthetic check through the full path, and a power check that must reject. The first run stopped at the power gate because the severity threshold was analytically mis-calibrated; the amendment corrected it, was recorded before any real-data metric, table, or verdict was computed or read, and left the base prereg byte-identical. The frequency arm had already rejected.

The arrival set, ten weeks around the 2020 crash, was pre-registered from the bare covariance before the dial, and the honesty block states that any variant-selection conclusion rests on the full-era metrics with the arrival set reported alongside, because selecting on ten famous weeks is overfitting to history’s most-studied episode.

Outcome

Result: the mixture does not earn the scenario job; the arrival gap is real for every variant; and the generator was mis-wired.

  • Audition. On the pooled primary portfolio the mixture did not beat the single-regime Student-t on the pre-registered PIT and severity tests; ties go to the simpler machinery by precedent. The same held under zero drift.
  • Arrival. Every variant, including the jump overlay, pins the same three crash-core weeks beyond its cloud at realized losses a multiple of predicted CVaR. A market-beta jump is still a draw through stable betas, the wrong object for a correlation-structure break. The arrival-gap conditions were not jointly met, so the study characterizes and issues no gap verdict.
  • The mis-wiring. The cloud-to-predictive dispersion guardrail showed the production mixture and its filtered variant running narrower than the certified one-step predictive they sit above, badly so in the selection era: the generator still drew its component covariances from the longer-window regime pool that the window study had retired from the predictive path. That understated CVaR (a many-but-mild breach pattern in the selection era) and handicapped the audition.
  • Drift sensitivity and portfolio robustness rows were reported; the weekly tail conclusions did not hinge on drift. The multi-period read showed the mixture ahead while handicapped, which the rebase later inverted.

Decision

No engine change from this study. Two follow-ups were routed: fix the wiring and re-run the audition clean (rebase), and characterize the position-level answer to arrival (overlay frontier). The Markov machinery’s fourth audition (weekly tail mass) is recorded here as a tie to the simpler null.

Where it led

The rebase corrected the base, resolved the breach pattern, and found the mixture’s remaining multi-period claim was an artifact of the mis-wiring, which led to the generator simplification.

Scenario-layer rebase: the mis-wiring resolved and the audition re-run clean (breach pattern resolved; the mixture’s last edge inverts)

Logged 2026-07-22.

A pre-registered wiring-alignment study that re-expressed the regime mixture’s component covariances on the certified predictive base, so the one-step scenario marginal equals the frozen predictive exactly, then re-ran the contaminated audition clean. The selection-era breach pattern was resolved, and the mixture’s prior multi-period edge turned out to be an artifact of the mis-wiring.

The bug (established by the audit, never rescored here)

When the window study replaced the predictive covariance and “retained the regime mixture for the scenario generator”, the generator’s component covariances were never re-based: it kept drawing from the longer-window regime pool. The scenario-layer audit measured the consequence: the scenario cloud ran narrower than the certified one-step predictive it sits above, worst in the selection era, so scenario CVaR was understated, the mixture showed a many-but-mild breach pattern, and the mixture-versus-null audition was handicapped.

Pre-registration (a wiring-alignment study, not an open research question)

The fix. A scale-preserving congruence rebase: subtract the between-regime mean term from the frozen predictive to get the target; map each state’s covariance through the Cholesky ratio of target to old pool; the mixture keeps its relative state structure while the one-step scenario marginal equals the frozen certified predictive to machine precision. Drift, degrees of freedom, the regime model, and the copula machinery unchanged; the old path preserved behind a configuration flag.

Phases. Freeze (write and hash the prereg, refusing silent overwrite); gate (the Phase-1 walk over the full history computing only the analytic one-step identity at the reproduction-gate standard and the per-era median cloud-to-predictive dispersion ratio, with no tail metric computed or read in this mode); then the full run: verify the prereg, run controls, re-verify the gate, score the pre-registered runs, verdict, report.

Established findings honored, not re-litigated. The mixture beat the null on multi-period dispersion while handicapped; weekly tail verdicts are drift-insensitive; the D1 drift is required for multi-period calibration; and the three crash-core arrival weeks are beyond every variant’s cloud and were not expected to be fixed. A rebase that “fixed” them would indicate a wiring error, a hard stop, not an improvement.

Governed amendment. The first full run stopped at the controls gate because the power check’s severity arm assumed a baseline near one while that seed’s synthetic stream drew its known-good baseline lower; the amendment made the severity arm relative to the run’s own known-good baseline, with the frequency arm decisive. Recorded after the controls readout and before any real-data tail metric was computed or read; base prereg byte-identical.

Outcome

Phase-1 gate: PASS. The analytic identity held across every week and variant at the reproduction-gate standard, and the per-era median dispersion ratios sat inside the band, against the mis-wired values well below it.

Verdicts, pre-registered logic printed explicitly.

  • (a) Breach pattern RESOLVED. The rebased mixture’s selection-era breach frequency fell to an interval containing the nominal level, with pooled severity containing one.
  • (b) The mixture’s multi-period edge EVAPORATES. While handicapped, the narrow pool had masked the mixture’s over-dispersion. Unhandicapped, the rebased mixture’s multi-week clouds are wider than realized dispersion warrants and the paired bootstrap intervals are clear in the single-regime null’s favor at both horizons. Said plainly in the record: the prior edge was an artifact of the mis-wiring. Recommended follow-up: simplify the generator to the single-regime iterated Student-t, as a separate decision.
  • (c) Weekly-tail conclusion UNCHANGED. The rebased mixture still does not beat the single-regime null; ties go to the simpler machinery, as in the audit.
  • Arrival UNCHANGED, as pre-registered. The same three crash-core weeks remain beyond every rebased cloud; the hard wiring check passed.

A known limitation was documented rather than retuned: the frozen generator’s interpolated standardized-Student-t quantile inflates the far tail at low degrees of freedom, beyond the resolution of the scored statistics and in the conservative direction; moot for the generator that later replaced it.

Decision

The rebase was deployed behind its flag (rollback to the old pool retained), defaulted on only after the Phase-1 gate passed and the Phase-2 tables were written. The walk-forward confirmation script was extended to score the rebased layer’s weekly CVaR breach on post-freeze weeks, hash-verified, refusing refit, with the identity asserted every scored week. The Markov machinery’s fifth audition, multi-period dispersion, its last measured claim, is recorded here as inverted.

Where it led

The recorded follow-up was executed the same day: the generator simplification retired the mixture.

Scenario generator simplified to a single-regime iterated Student-t (R-M1 deployed; the regime mixture retired to rollback)

Logged 2026-07-22.

A pre-registered decision study with zero new scored audition runs: every decision metric was reloaded bit-for-bit from the rebase study, and the only new computation was a reproduction gate proving the engine’s new generator path is the scored path. Non-inferior on every registered metric, the single-regime generator was deployed and the regime mixture now survives nowhere in the production model.

Question

The rebase removed the mixture’s handicap and its last measured claim inverted, while the weekly audition tied to the simpler machinery twice. The regime mixture earned no measured keep anywhere in the scenario layer. The recorded follow-up: simplify the generator to a single-regime iterated Student-t on the frozen certified predictive, retiring the mixture. Should it be deployed?

Pre-registration (a wiring gate plus a non-inferiority decision)

Zero new scored audition runs. The audition evidence already existed on the full grid in the rebase artifact, where the single-regime candidate was scored under the identical protocol, same weeks, same portfolios, same metrics as the rebased mixture. Every decision metric was reloaded bit-for-bit and never rescored. The only new computation was the reproduction gate.

The candidate. Single-regime iterated Student-t draws from the frozen one-step certified predictive, with the scenario mean set to the D1 mean-preserving split’s location (under a single regime the split degenerates to its mixture mean): the location is retained and the mixture’s skew content leaves with the mixture. The one-step marginal equals the certified predictive exactly by construction through the Student-t scale. The rebased mixture stays as the rollback.

Hard gate. G1: the engine’s weekly draw path reproduces the frozen rebase arrays for the candidate (every statistic, every probe portfolio, both drift rows, every week) at zero deviation. G2: the engine’s compounding path reproduces the multi-period arrays at zero deviation. G3: with the flag off, the rebased-mixture path is bit-identical to the pre-edit engine against a recorded hash. Fail any gate and the study stops, the flag stays off, the mixture stays.

Non-inferiority, pre-registered, ties or better deploy. N1 weekly: the mixture does not beat the candidate (reloaded verdict), the candidate’s breach-frequency intervals contain nominal and its severity interval contains one, and the PIT differences sit inside a small margin. N2 multi-period: the paired dispersion intervals favor the candidate or tie. N3 arrival: the pinned arrival set is unchanged. If the candidate were not non-inferior on any metric, stop and leave the mixture in place.

Outcome

Result: all gates PASS; non-inferior on every metric; DEPLOY.

  • The engine reproduced the frozen weekly and multi-period arrays at zero deviation, and the flag-off path was bit-identical to the recorded hash.
  • N1 passed; N2’s paired intervals were not merely non-inferior but strictly in the candidate’s favor at both horizons; N3 passed with the same crash-core weeks pinned beyond the extreme shell.

Decision

The production scenario generator is the single-regime iterated Student-t on the frozen certified predictive, behind the generator flag, with the rebased mixture as the tested rollback. The walk-forward confirmation script scores the deployed generator and the rollback on post-freeze weeks, refusing to score the deployed row unless the gate and the decision are on record as passed. What is given up, stated plainly in the record: regime-persistence path clustering and the D1 skew placement, which bought no measured win on the full grid, and the arrival gap was never covered by either generator.

The regime mixture now survives nowhere in the production model. This closes the arc that began with the Markov build: the machinery lost five auditions on the record, as a clustering fix (regime battery), as the level conditioner (conditional level correction), as predictive pooling (window study), as weekly tail mass (scenario audit), and on multi-period dispersion (rebase). The empirical case it was built on, that correlations re-wire in crisis, still stands; no implementation of it earned a production keep, and the crash-arrival blind spot it was aimed at is a sizing question (overlay frontier), not a regime-machinery one. The Markov code remains as research and tested-rollback machinery only. The Current Model page records the deployed generator.

Correction (2026-11-17). “The regime mixture now survives nowhere in the production model” and “The Markov code remains as research and tested-rollback machinery only” are not accurate: the regime model still sets the active universe and produces the displayed portfolio volatility (and the ex-ante Sharpe ratio computed from it) and the factor variance shares behind the 0.35 dRATE cap. Where it led
