Single-use holdout: the pre-registered claim did not hold

The pre-registered single-use evaluation of the frozen engine did not reproduce the tail under-reservation it was meant to confirm, and the benchmark beat the allocator.
Published

July 20, 2026

Study Date Status Reason (from its record)
Single-use holdout of the merged engine 2026-07-20 rejected “The primary pre-registered hypothesis failed to reproduce” (holdout evaluation record)

Over the holdout the benchmark (risk parity) was ahead of STARR, the frozen engine’s allocator, on returns in excess over T-bills: its Sharpe ratio on those returns was higher at every cost scale. The holdout was the pre-registered, run-once evaluation of the frozen merged engine on a sealed span; its primary question was whether the tail under-reservation found on the selection span would reproduce there. It did not, and the record reports that as a negative result; breach frequency was right, risk earned qualified trust and the allocator did not, and the holdout is consumed. The sealed record scored Sharpe with rf = 0; a dated addendum of 2026-11-16 re-reads it on returns in excess over T-bills (the 4-month bill is the rate of record) without rescoring anything, and the sealed record is untouched. A second dated addendum of 2026-11-17 corrects the first: it had taken each period’s bill rate four weeks early, so its excess-return Sharpe ratios were about 0.016 too high; the conclusion is unchanged.

Single-use holdout of the merged engine (spent; tail under-reservation did not reproduce, the benchmark beat STARR)

Logged 2026-07-20.

The pre-registered, run-once evaluation of the frozen merged engine on the sealed span. The primary hypothesis did not reproduce, breach frequency was right, one clustering rejection appeared, and risk parity beat the production allocator on return, Sharpe, and drawdown. Reported as-is; the holdout is consumed.

What the harness is

A span-generalized walk-forward plus a scoring layer, written so the same code runs twice. First, a dry run on the selection span, which the harness is already allowed to see, that must reproduce the engine’s own cost-aware walk-forward to the last decimal; the engine’s function is left untouched as an independent reference, so agreement is real evidence that the scoring code has no off-by-one, no look-ahead in the predicted-versus-realized alignment, and the right horizon. Second, the holdout itself, run once and reported as-is. Nothing reads holdout data unless the explicit flag is passed and the run-once sentinel is absent; a second call refuses. Every input artifact was re-hashed against the data manifest before scoring, and an out-of-band snapshot archive with a restore test was taken.

Pre-registration (written before the holdout was opened)

Frozen. The regime-conditional hybrid covariance on the longer lookback the engine shipped with, the two-state model with the incumbent signal and filtered probabilities, a single global degrees of freedom within regime, the strategic consensus expected returns with the keep-fraction and the bond yield anchor, the D1 drift, the stationary regime-mixture generator, the STARR allocator with risk-parity benchmark and mean-variance overlay, the two clocks, tiered one-way costs at three scales, and the conservatism multiplier with the raw default shipped and the recommended buffer documented. The multiplier was the one to watch: frozen precisely so the holdout could not pick it.

Exactly how each number is computed. Decision dates on the four-week clock with no partial final month; strictly causal estimation at each date; scenarios scored against the return over the following four weeks only; weights drifting between rebalances; costs charged only where the threshold fires. The active universe changes mid-holdout, pre-registered here rather than explained afterward: the managed-futures name reaches the required lookback partway through and enters, with a turnover blip that is not to be reported as a pathology.

Reported metrics. Tail calibration on the equal-weight and risk-parity probes (breach definition, the ES-to-CVaR ratio, at both multiplier settings, pooled and split calm versus crisis); Kupiec and Christoffersen coverage tests; net performance per sleeve and cost scale; pathology (max weight, dominant asset, breadth, names at the cap, the stress-window drawdown). Every outcome had a pre-registered reading, including “the benchmark is allowed to win” and “a negative result is reported honestly and the buffer is not revised to fit”. Sample-size honesty stated in advance: a handful of expected breaches per probe makes this a directional confirm, not a measurement.

Four gates, in order. Pre-registration written; dry-run reproduction exact; user review and explicit go; run once. A buggy harness burns the holdout as permanently as tuning does.

Outcome (consumed 2026-07-20, against the frozen engine commit)

Result: the primary hypothesis did not reproduce; risk earns qualified trust; the allocator does not.

  • Tail under-reservation did not reproduce. Pooled ES-to-CVaR at the shipped multiplier did not exceed one on either probe, and the crisis blow-out seen on the selection span did not recur. Per the pre-registered table this is the negative-result branch: the recommended buffer stays documented and unactivated, and is not revised downward to fit. Shipping the raw number was vindicated: a baked-in buffer would have made the holdout measure a buffered figure instead of true calibration.
  • Breach frequency is correct. Coverage rejects nowhere; slightly fewer breaches than nominal, in the conservative direction.
  • One genuine negative. Independence rejects on the risk-parity probe pooled: the two breaches were adjacent months. The pre-registered reading is that the regime model is not absorbing time variation as intended. With two breaches the test is very low-powered, which weakens confidence in the rejection and does not excuse it; flagged for follow-up.
  • Risk parity beat STARR out of sample on every dimension: higher return, higher risk-adjusted return, and a smaller drawdown. The drawdown is the sharper strike, because STARR is the tail-aware allocator and took the larger worst drawdown on exactly that axis.
  • No pathology. Breadth held, roughly one name at the cap, small cost drag. The regime path flagged the 2022 bear as crisis at both re-estimations spanning it. The universe expansion occurred as pre-registered.

Decision

The holdout is consumed and cannot be reused. Any future change to the allocator or the multiplier must be justified on new out-of-sample data or on reasoning, never by re-running this window. Only after this event did live estimation through the latest date become the standing production behavior, the sequence discipline the spec records. Honest scope: one roughly four-year sample with one major stress episode is enough to say the tail is not dangerously under-reserved and that STARR did not earn its keep here, and not enough to estimate any tail ratio precisely or to conclude STARR is structurally inferior.
