The distribution-calibration battery (standing harness and change protocol)

Logged 2026-07-20.

The estimator-agnostic whitening and PIT battery that became the standard acceptance test for any covariance or predictive change: Cholesky whitening on the model’s own covariance, a dimension-free PIT ruler, coverage and independence tests, and negative controls that must pass before any real result is read.

What it is

A self-contained, estimator-agnostic battery that answers one question: given a sequence of realized return vectors and a model’s predicted covariance for each week, are the realized returns statistically consistent with being one-step-ahead draws from the model’s predictive with zero mean?

The whole test lives on a dimension-free ruler, so it needs no shared factor basis and tolerates a universe whose active count changes as assets enter and leave:

  • Whiten each week’s return by the model’s own covariance, by Cholesky forward-solve, never an eigen inverse square root.
  • Mahalanobis distance of the whitened vector, chi-squared under a Gaussian null.
  • PIT of that distance, uniform under the null and a scalar regardless of dimension, so the Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Berkowitz, and Christoffersen tests run on it directly.
  • Bias ratio, the root-mean of the distance per dimension, one under the null: above one is under-reserved, below one over-reserved.

Mean-zero track. Nothing here estimates a mean. Any mean fitted to realized returns would compress the residual and flatter a broken covariance, so the battery refuses to touch it. It is a covariance and predictive-distribution test, full stop.

Standard definitions, reimplemented from the textbook. A second-moment readout; Kolmogorov-Smirnov on the distance PIT and on pooled whitened components; the Berkowitz likelihood ratio against an autoregressive alternative; Kupiec unconditional coverage plus Christoffersen independence and conditional coverage on the exceedance sequence. A positive-definiteness floor fires only when a covariance is singular, and is counted and flagged.

Negative controls first. The battery simulates a known-good case (draw from the true covariance, whiten by it) that it must pass, plus a mis-specified case and a volatility-clustering case that it must reject, proving the tests have power. A failed control is an integration bug, not a model finding.

The change protocol it anchors

The battery is the standard acceptance test for any covariance or predictive change. The rules that travel with it:

  • Whiten on the model’s own covariance in asset space, the object the optimizer inverts.
  • For Student-t, copula, or mixture predictives use the empirical PIT (a fixed number of causal draws from the model’s own predictive each week, validated by a mixture control), never the chi-squared PIT, which would wrongly reject a correct fat-tailed model. The empirical PIT lives in the regime battery module.
  • Rank ladders by statistic, not p-value: under residual dependence the nominal p-values are anti-conservative and saturate.
  • Controls first; stop on failure.
  • Five pre-registered steps for any model change: a contiguous three-way temporal split with a structurally sealed holdout whose loader cannot run before the freeze record exists; pre-registration of the full variant list before any out-of-sample evaluation, immutable afterward; a trial budget with every evaluation logged, Bonferroni correction on the winner, and paired stationary-bootstrap intervals at the corrected quantile; a negative control that runs the identical selection on a serial-dependence-destroyed panel; and selection on worst-sub-window stability rather than the peak point estimate.
  • A winner must improve both the clustering metric and the level metric, survive all five steps, and hold on the holdout. A clean negative that survives the protocol is a valid, publishable outcome; do not manufacture a winner.

Where it led

The battery was first run on the live pipeline machinery for the six single-regime estimators (weekly calibration battery), then carried, verbatim, through every study of the risk-model calibration program, the scenario program, and the full-model assessment. It is the ruler every verdict in this log’s risk-model and scenario-layer categories was measured with.

Weekly calibration battery for six single-regime estimators (all six reject independence; the first reading was superseded)

Logged 2026-07-20.

The first walk-forward whitening and PIT battery on the live data machinery: every single-regime covariance estimator rejected exceedance independence. It was first read as fresh-angle evidence for the regime switch; that attribution did not survive the follow-up studies, and the record marks it superseded.

Question

How well is each covariance estimator’s predicted covariance calibrated one week ahead on real weekly data, isolated from expected returns, on the dimension-free PIT ruler of the calibration battery? Because the test lives in asset space there is no forced common factor basis, so a rotating active universe is handled exactly.

Design

Single-regime by design. The six estimators were applied without regime conditioning: one trailing-window covariance per week, mixed regimes. The script’s original reading, recorded before the follow-ups: a constant or trailing-window covariance cannot track the volatility clustering in mixed-regime data, so the Christoffersen independence test was expected to reject for most or all estimators, and that rejection would be direct, from-a-fresh-angle evidence for the regime switch that lives downstream.

The six estimators, verbatim transforms from the regime notebook: the raw trailing sample covariance; shrinkage toward constant correlation; the split clock (slow correlation times a fast volatility clock); the random-matrix eigenvalue-clipped correlation; a library Ledoit-Wolf benchmark; and the hybrid factor model.

Guardrails asserted at run time. Every covariance estimated from a window ending strictly before the scored week; horizon equals step equals one week, non-overlapping; finite, symmetric, positive semi-definite every week, with the idiosyncratic floor firing only on a non-positive-definite matrix and counted; zero mean. The walk-forward defaults to the selection span, ending where the single-use holdout starts.

Outcome

Result: all six single-regime estimators reject exceedance independence. Exceptions cluster. The split clock was the worst row of all six, with a bias ratio far above one and violations many times expectation, reconfirming its earlier rejection. The library shrinkage benchmark showed the least clustering and was briefly crowned on that basis.

The superseded reading (kept for history, as the record keeps it)

The rejection was real; its attribution was not. The follow-up batteries showed the clustering is not fixed by regime or volatility dynamics of any flavor: exponentially weighted, dynamic-conditional-correlation, and the causal Markov mixture all still reject (regime battery). It is within-regime tail structure, eliminated by Student-t innovations on the slice study’s sealed holdout. The six-of-six rejection stands as evidence that the Gaussian predictive was wrong; its attribution to the missing regime switch did not survive. The shrinkage “least clustering” crown was also superseded: an over-reservation artifact (a low bias ratio means few hits, so little clustering evidence to detect), and calm-window-conditional.

Decision

No component changed. The battery became the standard acceptance test, and this run is the first step of the three-step evidence chain that made Student-t innovations the predictive standard for the whole risk model.

Where it led

The regime battery scored the Markov predictive on the same grid, same whitening, same ruler, against matched continuous-volatility baselines, to test the attribution this run had proposed.

NoteVerdict and decision of record: slice study

The slice study’s own report gives “Winner: NONE” and “Distributional fix works: NO”. The decision of record adopts Student-t innovations from it and rejects the constant level scale (model specification). The specification’s changelog gives the reason: the study returned a “formal NO WINNER under the pre-registered gates (a clean negative, reported as such)”, “but t innovations eliminate clustering on the holdout” and “the constant level scale fails the holdout gate”. A winner had to “improve both the clustering metric and the level metric” (model specification); the top candidate, Student-t innovations with a constant scale, failed on level, so the clustering finding was promoted on its own and the constant scale was rejected.

Slice study: within-regime tails and the level bias under a sealed holdout (NO WINNER; Student-t innovations adopted; constant level scale rejected)

Logged 2026-07-21.

The first fully governed study: two axes of variants (within-regime tail structure, factor-architecture level bias) under a pre-registered, budgeted, negative-controlled, sealed-holdout protocol. No variant cleared every gate. Two findings were promoted regardless: Student-t innovations eliminate the exception clustering, and no constant level scale fixes both regimes.

Question

Established, not re-derived: exception clustering persists identically across static, exponentially weighted, dynamic-correlation, and Markov factor dynamics (regime battery), so the residual dependence is not volatility dynamics; and a shared under-reservation bias rides on the hybrid architecture, not on the factor covariance. So: does within-regime tail structure (Axis A) fix the clustering, and does a level fix in the architecture (Axis B) fix the bias? Regime dynamics were out of scope as the primary search.

Pre-registration (the protocol, enforced structurally)

  1. A three-way contiguous temporal split, never shuffled: a training era, an out-of-sample era where all selection happens, and a holdout era sealed until the final step. The holdout loader raises unless the run-phase flag says holdout, and the flag flips only after the freeze record is written; the holdout week count is asserted up front from an index-only peek.
  2. The pre-registration written before any out-of-sample evaluation, with an immutable variant list whose hash a rerun verifies and aborts on mismatch.
  3. A trial budget, every out-of-sample evaluation appended to the trial log, Bonferroni correction on the winner’s tests, and paired stationary-bootstrap intervals at the corrected quantile for the bias and clustering improvements.
  4. A negative control: a seeded whole-week permutation of the panel that destroys serial dependence and preserves cross-sections and marginals exactly, run through the same selection machinery.
  5. Selection on stability: half-year sub-windows; the winner must improve in most of them and is chosen by its worst sub-window.
  6. Freeze, flip the phase flag, open the holdout once, run the battery for the winner and the frozen baseline only. No iteration after seeing it.

Variants. Axis A: Student-t innovations with causally estimated degrees of freedom, a Student-t copula, and a Markov mixture with Student-t; Axis B: an isotropic random-matrix treatment, several idiosyncratic-diagonal alternatives (exponentially weighted, long-window, shrunk), and a causal constant scale from the Wishart-degrees-of-freedom logic; plus combinations. Ground rules: zero mean; full-asset-space whitening on each variant’s own covariance; chi-squared PIT only for Gaussian single-component rows, empirical PIT for Student-t, copula, and mixture rows; strict causality asserted; deterministic seeds; positive-definiteness asserted with flooring flagged.

Outcome

Result: NO WINNER, a clean negative. The top candidate (Student-t innovations with a constant scale) passed the out-of-sample gates, the stability rule, and the null tripwire, but failed the Bonferroni bias band and the holdout level gate. Neither the distributional fix nor the architectural fix “works” under the full gate set, and no winner was manufactured.

Two findings were promoted to the spec regardless, because the record supports them independently of the winner rule:

  • Student-t innovations eliminate the exception clustering on the sealed holdout. The independence statistic collapsed to non-rejecting and exceptions fell to near expectation where the Gaussian baseline clustered badly. Fat margins matter: the Student-t copula alone barely moved the clustering, and the Gaussian mixture alone was again insufficient. Fat tails, not volatility dynamics, were the missing piece. Adopted as the predictive innovation distribution for the whole risk model, with degrees of freedom fit causally on the expanding pool of whitened residuals.
  • A constant level scale is rejected in any placement. Calibrated on the turbulent out-of-sample era, the constant scale over-reserved in the calm holdout era while the unscaled baseline sat closer to one. The level bias is regime-dependent with the dangerous ordering: most under-reserved in stress, least in calm. The correctly shaped instrument is state-dependent.

Decision

Student-t innovations became the documented predictive standard: scoring any Student-t or mixture predictive must use the empirical PIT. The constant scale went on the rejected list, do not re-introduce. The next study was written into the record: a state-dependent level correction conditioned on regime probability.

Contamination, disclosed. This study opened the holdout era once. Every later study in the program labels that era semi-clean or contaminated and gives it zero gate weight; the only clean confirmation channel is walk-forward on weeks accruing after each freeze.

Where it led

  • The conditional level-correction study tested the state-dependent scale with the Markov model as the candidate conditioner.
  • The tail-risk model test had reached the same “mixture plus within-regime fat tails” conclusion at the monthly horizon; this study reproduced it at weekly frequency under governance and localized the load-bearing part to the fat margins.
NoteVerdict and decision of record: conditional-c study

The conditional-c study’s own report gives “Winner: NONE” and “Conditional-c works: NO”. The decision of record adopts the V3 vol-state dial from it (model specification). The specification gives the reason under “Why V3 stands despite a formal NO WINNER”: V3 “fixed both buckets OOS”, kept clustering solved and passed its negative control, and “missed only the Bonferroni-corrected superiority bound”, which it calls “a power limit, not a sign it doesn’t work”; its reading is “no manufactured winner, but V3 is the settled instrument”. The clean confirmation the study designated, walk-forward scoring of weeks after the freeze, had no accrued weeks when the full-model assessment ran.

Conditional level correction: can the Markov model condition the level scale? (NO WINNER; Markov conditioner rejected; the vol-state dial V3 survives)

Logged 2026-07-21.

A pre-registered test of a state-dependent level scale on the adopted Student-t predictive, with the regime model auditioning as the conditioner. No variant cleared every gate. The Markov-conditioned forms failed their negative control; the causal volatility-state dial fixed both buckets, kept clustering solved, and was frozen as the top candidate.

Question

Established, not re-derived: the production predictive is the hybrid covariance with multivariate Student-t innovations and causal degrees of freedom; clustering is solved by the Student-t innovations and is guarded here, never selected on; the level bias is regime-dependent (under-reserved in stress, over-reserved in calm) and a constant scale is rejected. Hypothesis: the level correction must be state-dependent, because under-reservation concentrates in stress. Two questions: does a conditional level scale fix the regime-dependent bias, and does that give the Markov model a justified production role as the level conditioner?

Pre-registration (frozen before any out-of-sample evaluation)

Contamination disclosure, verbatim in the prereg. The conditional-scale hypothesis was formed by inspecting holdout-era summary statistics from the slice study. No parameters were fitted to that era, but it cannot serve as clean confirmation for this hypothesis. Therefore the functional forms were frozen before any fitting, the terminal window is labeled semi-clean in every table, and the designated clean confirmation is walk-forward evaluation on weeks accruing after the freeze, with a re-evaluation script that refuses to refit.

Split. Training era; an out-of-sample selection era that spans both a calm year and the stress years; a semi-clean terminal era opened once post-freeze behind a phase guard.

Variants. The baseline (which must reproduce the slice study’s Student-t arrays bit-for-bit, asserted); two Markov forms conditioning the scale on the filtered or predicted regime probability; V3, a volatility-state dial, the exponential of an intercept plus a slope times a causal thirteen-week realized-volatility z-score of the market factor, with the two coefficients fit by causal regression on training weeks only; and an idiosyncratic-diagonal channel. Buckets defined by causal predicted stress probability.

Correction (2026-11-17). The record has the dial’s two coefficients fit by causal expanding regression over the earlier weeks, not on training weeks only; the frozen coefficients are the last such fit (conditional-scale study pre-registration; confirmation run).

Gates. A raw-band non-rejection gate on both buckets; a corrected improvement gate on the worst-bucket statistic; a clustering guard (a level fix must not unsolve the tails); a shape gate; and a negative control in which the conditioning series is block-scrambled through the same machinery and real conditioning must beat scrambled by a margin (a scrambled conditioner collapses to a near-constant scale, so the tripwire separates true state dependence from the already-rejected constant effect). Stability on half-year worst-bucket statistics, most consistent wins. A trial budget with every scored configuration logged.

Outcome

Result: NO WINNER, a clean negative. Conditional scale under the full gate set: no. Markov earns the conditioner job: no.

  • The Markov-conditioned forms fixed the stress bucket but over-corrected the calm bucket, failed the corrected improvement gate, and, decisively, failed the negative control: a block-scrambled regime series beat the real one. Crisis-scale magnitudes learned from one crisis did not transfer to the next.
  • The idiosyncratic-diagonal channel worsened both buckets: the wrong channel entirely.
  • V3 fixed both out-of-sample buckets, kept clustering solved, passed its negative control (real conditioning beat scrambled), landed inside the non-rejection band, and held on the semi-clean era. It missed only the Bonferroni-corrected superiority bound on a short out-of-sample sample, a power limit rather than a sign it does not work. Labeled the top candidate, non-winning, and frozen with its coefficients and degrees of freedom in the study artifact.
  • The clustering guardrail held for every variant not vetoed.

Correction (2026-11-17). The first bullet has the buckets the wrong way round: out of sample the Markov-conditioned forms brought the calm bucket inside its band (bias 0.95 and 1.00) and over-corrected the stress bucket (0.78 and 0.70, with bands that exclude 1) (conditional-scale study report).

Decision

Nothing was adopted by this study. V3 was designated for clean confirmation on post-freeze weeks via the walk-forward script, and the Markov model’s audition as the level conditioner was recorded as failed, the second of five.

Where it led

  • Three further studies tried to fix the level bias at its source so that the V3 “patch” could be deleted, and each failed, leaving V3 as the efficient reader of the mechanism they uncovered: the architecture study, the window-lag study, and the daily realized-variance study.
  • The final confirmation measured the cost of leaving the dial unwired, and the window study wired it into the production predictive covariance, with the coefficients read frozen from this study’s artifact at runtime and never hard-coded. The standing watch item is the dial’s calm-bucket bias on accruing clean weeks; the pre-agreed response is a one-parameter recalibration under a new small prereg, not a new hypothesis.

Architecture study: is the level bias Marchenko-Pastur truncation loss? (HYPOTHESIS WRONG)

Logged 2026-07-21.

A pre-registered disconfirmation test of whether the systematic level bias lives in the factor architecture, specifically the random-matrix truncation of the residual correlation, so that fixing it at the source would make the V3 dial deletable. The trace-level version was already wrong at the source, and the surviving off-diagonal version failed every gate.

Correction (2026-11-17). “Failed every gate” is not accurate: the isotropic add-back failed the band gate and the negative control, and the raw-bulk variant failed the band, improvement and shape gates (architecture study report); neither was eligible.

Question

Does the systematic level bias live in the factor architecture, specifically the Marchenko-Pastur truncation of the residual correlation, and does fixing it at the source remove the need for any conditional level multiplier, the V3 dial from the conditional level-correction study?

Pre-registration (frozen before any out-of-sample touch)

Central hypothesis, registered verbatim and falsifiable. The random-matrix cutoff discards eigenvalue mass from the residual correlation estimate; that mass is variance the model never reserves for, and it is larger in high-volatility windows. Predictions: restoring the truncated mass moves the bias ratio toward one in both regime buckets (P1); the volatility-conditional signature shrinks, the calm/stress gap narrows, and a V3-style refit on top of the fix yields a slope near zero, nothing left to patch (P2); clustering stays solved, or the variant is disqualified (P3). If P1 and P2 fail, the architecture story is wrong and the honest fallback is V3 as a patch pending its walk-forward.

Step 0 discovery, verified numerically before anything ran. Production builds the idiosyncratic diagonal as the residual variance minus the diagonal of the retained signal part, clipped. So the eigenvalue mass below the cutoff is not discarded at the trace level: its per-asset diagonal is folded into the idiosyncratic block. What production discards is only the off-diagonal structure of the truncated subspace. Consequences frozen before scoring: the diagonal add-back is analytically identical to production and can only serve as a wiring check; the trace-level reading of the hypothesis is already disconfirmed at the source; the study proceeds as the pre-registered disconfirmation, testing the surviving off-diagonal version (isotropic clipping to the bulk mean, or keeping the raw bulk).

Protocol. Training era; out-of-sample selection era with both volatility states asserted present; the terminal era demoted as contaminated twice (the slice holdout and the conditional-scale semi-clean read), reported for continuity with no gate weight and kept sealed behind the run-phase guard regardless. Variants: the baseline (must reproduce the frozen arrays bit-for-bit), the isotropic add-back, the diagonal add-back (null), the raw bulk, the V3 patch benchmark reloaded frozen and never rescored, and a probe that refits a V3-style dial on top of the add-back. Gates as in the program, plus a negative control that block-scrambles the bulk mass series (right amounts, wrong times). A trial budget with every configuration logged.

Outcome

Result: HYPOTHESIS WRONG. Eligible after gates: none. Winner: none. No add-back beats the V3 patch.

  • Step 0 proved per-asset variance is preserved exactly; the “discarded mass” claim was disconfirmed at the source. The truncated mass is diagonalized, not deleted.
  • The surviving off-diagonal leak is small and, decisively, anti-correlated with volatility: the conditionality runs the wrong way, so restoring it cannot move a volatility-conditional bias.
  • The isotropic add-back improved the out-of-sample point estimate but failed the band gate and was flagged by the negative control: its improvement is a level shift in disguise, with the conditional signature caused by something else. The raw-bulk variant failed the shape gate. Conditioning was not improved.
  • The P2 probe. The refit dial slope on the add-back’s history stayed significantly positive, essentially unchanged from the baseline. The dial still has its full job to do on top of the architectural fix.
  • The clustering guardrail held for every scored variant.

Decision

Nothing changed. The Marchenko-Pastur truncation went on the rejected list as the level-bias cause, and the record now states the architecture fact plainly: truncated mass is diagonalized into the idiosyncratic block by construction.

Where it led

The level bias had to have another mechanism. The window-lag study named it: estimation-window lag in the factor block.

Window-lag study: is the level bias estimation-window lag, and can reweighting fix it at the source? (NOT CONFIRMED: the lag is real, the fix is not)

Logged 2026-07-21.

A pre-registered test of whether the volatility-conditional level bias is the equal-weight window mechanically under-estimating current variance whenever volatility sits above its trailing mean, and whether a properly damped weighting removes it. The lag is real and quantified; no weighting speed removes it without re-breaking clustering. V3 is the efficient use of the lag signal.

Question

Is the volatility-conditional level bias caused by estimation-window lag in the factor block, the equal-weight window estimating average variance over several years and therefore under-estimating current variance whenever volatility sits above its trailing mean, and does a properly damped exponentially weighted or volatility-targeted factor-block weighting remove it at the source, making the V3 dial deletable?

Pre-registration (frozen before any out-of-sample touch)

Central hypothesis, registered verbatim. The equal-weight trailing window estimates average variance; when current volatility exceeds its trailing mean, the definition of stress, the factor-block estimate is mechanically too small in proportion to the volatility z-score. This is why V3’s conditioning variable works: it is the analytic antidote to window lag. Predictions: volatility-responsive factor-block weighting moves both buckets toward one, including the stress bucket every prior fix missed (P1); a V3-style refit on top of the winner yields a slope near zero, the dial deletable (P2); clustering stays solved, with the causal degrees of freedom refit per variant because faster windows change residual kurtosis (P3); the improvement operates through timing, so a timing-scrambled weighting must lose it (P4).

Prior art, registered. The split clock already tried fast volatility tracking and failed badly: over-reaction and noise dominated, not lag. The slice study tested exponential weighting only on the idiosyncratic diagonal, never the factor block. So the open question was whether a properly damped weighting beats both the equal-weight lag and the split clock’s noise; the speed grid brackets that damping range deliberately, and selection is on stability (most consistent worst-bucket statistic, not the best point) to avoid re-committing the split clock’s error.

Consistency rule, registered. The idiosyncratic diagonal is always rebuilt by the production rule using the same weighting as the variant’s factor block; every downstream object responds through the existing construction. No variant may hand-tune the diagonal.

Protocol. Training era; out-of-sample era with both buckets asserted present; the terminal era contaminated three times over, zero gate weight, loader sealed until the freeze record exists. Variants: the baseline, two damped exponentially weighted speeds, a faster speed, a volatility-targeted factor-block diagonal, a configuration with a dial refit on the best damped variant, and the V3 comparator reloaded frozen. Gates as in the program plus a timing-scramble negative control and a flooring gate (routine flooring disqualifies). A trial budget with every configuration logged.

Outcome

Result: NOT CONFIRMED, the middle case. The lag is real; the fix is not.

  • Step 0 quantified the lag before scoring.
