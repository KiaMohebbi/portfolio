This program asked what the model portfolio’s expected returns should be built from, and how much of their structure holds up under causal, pre-registered tests. The original layer projected consensus capital-market assumptions through the frozen factor loadings; the studies that followed found that what validated was the bond yields and the ordering of risk assets above defensives (an ordering the records call epoch-dependent), so the within-sleeve cross-section was retired and the expected returns became sleeve-level. A factor-implied cross-section and observable anchors for the alternative sleeves then failed their tests, while a tilt whose shape comes from the certified covariance passed every gate and was adopted on top of the sleeve-level base. A closing measurement found no measurable mean-reversion effect, and the adopted construction stands as the expected returns of record. One question about it is open and not started (model specification, open decisions, item 13): with the T-bill rate in the STARR objective, the max-STARR tangency on the 2026-07-17 snapshot sits next to the minimum-CVaR end of the frontier, which the specification suspects is an artifact of these expected returns, since they price low-beta risky names well above the bill. The delivered rule holds these expected returns fixed and does not close it.

Expected returns build: consensus CMAs projected through the frozen loadings (built; later simplified)

Logged 2026-07-18.

The original mu layer: factor premia backed out of forward-looking consensus capital-market assumptions through the frozen hybrid loadings, an implied-alpha keep-fraction chosen by cross-sectional rank IC, and a yield-anchored bond sleeve. The ranking and the bond level were tested; the equity level cannot be.

What this built

The strategic, ten-year, unconditional expected-return vector for the universe, built by projecting consensus capital-market assumptions through the same frozen factor loadings the risk model uses. Everything else was frozen: the weekly spliced data, the hybrid loadings from the factor-model variants test, the regime model, the tail model, and the holdout.

The core principle: expected returns and covariance are not symmetric. For covariance, realized factor returns are observed every week, so second moments are an estimation problem solved with more data. For expected returns the need is factor premia, and means cannot be estimated from historical averages; that is the sample-mean error-maximizer the whole consensus approach exists to avoid (means need calendar span, not observation count). So the projection machinery mirrors risk, through the same loadings, but the inputs are forward-looking consensus premia, never historical factor means.

Construction and locked design

  • Factor premia backed out of the consensus vector by generalized least squares through the frozen loadings, with weights that down-weight buckets whose assumptions are reasoned fills rather than genuinely published consensus (an honesty flag carried per bucket).
  • Only the observable macro factors are priced. The latent random-matrix factors are real for covariance but their premia are scale-arbitrary and flip sign under reweighting; they are reported and never routed into expected returns.
  • The implied alpha is the residual each assumption leaves unexplained; a keep-fraction sets how much of it survives, between pure projection (maximum denoising) and trusting every bucket number literally.
  • The bond sleeve gets a per-ticker starting-yield override rather than the shared bucket assumption, which pulled the over-stated long-duration numbers down.
  • Geometric consensus figures converted to arithmetic; a ten-year strategic horizon, not a one-year forecast; Black-Litterman deferred with the explicit-alpha seam left in place; a custom-strategy hook that prices any strategy’s betas through the same premia.

Tests (design fixed up front; show the work)

  • Test 1, cross-sectional rank IC (keep-fraction selection). Pure projection roughly doubled the out-of-sample ranking power of the raw consensus cross-section; the IC was flat over the low end of the keep-fraction range, so the upper end of that flat band was chosen to retain as much deliberate cross-section as the test permits. Only a small share of the consensus cross-section is factor-explained; the rest is deliberate bucket alpha, which ranks better when treated as un-rankable, so genuine conviction belongs in the views layer, not a higher keep-fraction. Passed.
  • Test 2, bond yield anchoring. Realized long-horizon return tracks starting yield almost one for one. Passed.
  • Test 3, equity level bias. Overlapping long-horizon windows leave roughly one effective observation. Underpowered by construction; selects nothing.
  • Test 4, consistency. Premia signs economically right; implied alpha small and mean-near-zero; premia stable across windows. Passed.

Outcome and the honesty block

Result: built and merged. What was tested and passed: the cross-sectional ranking and the bond-sleeve level. What was not and cannot be tested: the equity level, which rests on consensus faith by construction. The record’s own warning, kept verbatim in spirit: a good backtest does not mean the expected returns were right; the portfolio backtest validates the allocation machinery, never the assumptions; and no equity curve may launder into “the return assumptions were validated”.

Regime-conditional expected returns were rejected as market timing with the wrong sign (realized crisis returns are the premium being paid out, not evidence forward premia fell). Historical factor means for the premia went on the rejected list as the error-maximizer in factor costume.

Where it led

The mu program that followed re-examined every component of this layer under governed, causal reconstruction. The rank-IC study reinterpreted Test 1 as a keep-fraction selection curve rather than validation and found the validated content is sleeve-level only, with the within-sleeve ordering indistinguishable-to-inverted under causal loadings. The bond-sleeve level study confirmed the yield anchor at the level and found the machinery beyond the raw yield earns nothing. The mu simplification then retired the cross-section and the keep-fraction (retired, not condemned) while keeping the premia machinery for the custom-strategy hook. This layer is preserved bit-for-bit as the deeper rollback of the running model; see the Current Model page.

Mu-perturbation sensitivity: how much do the shipped decisions move under plausible expected-return error? (descriptive; composition-fragile, risk-firewalled)

Logged 2026-07-23.

The opening characterization of the expected-returns program. Plausible error in the expected-return layer moves a large share of the book and flips top holdings, yet leaves tail risk pinned by the CVaR budget and realized outcomes unchanged at the median. The plausible error is the same size as the whole signal; the bond yield anchor is the highest-leverage input.

Question

How sensitive are the shipped optimizer’s decisions (weights, risk profile, realized outcome) to plausible errors in the expected-return layer? Descriptive: no adoption, no winner, no engine change, read-only on the engine.

Pre-registration (hash written before any scoring)

Decision object: the shipped portfolio. The live allocation selects the CVaR-efficient frontier point nearest a monthly CVaR budget (maximize expected return subject to the budget, fully invested, long-only, per-asset cap), the point from the frontier machinery. In most evaluation weeks the budget binds; in a few deep-volatility weeks the minimum feasible CVaR exceeds it and the shipped point is the leftmost point, the faithful nearest-available rule. The STARR tangency sits essentially at minimum CVaR and is expected-return-insensitive; risk parity is expected-return-invariant by construction, the robustness floor.

Faithful injection. Perturb the belief (the consensus vector for the projected sleeve, the yield anchor for the bond sleeve) and re-derive the final expected returns through the exact production construction, reproduced to machine zero. Scenarios held fixed at each week’s base cloud, so a perturbation is a pure objective swap over a fixed feasible set.

Perturbation families at a plausible-error unit anchored to the data (the median of the rolling realized-mean-versus-anchor error and the standard error of a multi-year mean), run at half, one, and two units: independent noise; factor-aligned (shared) error; a common level shift; an adjacent-rank scramble that preserves magnitudes; and error in the bond yield-anchor sleeve. A shrinkage-interaction sweep. A representative panel of evaluation weeks split calm, mid, and stress by the frozen volatility z-score. Metrics: weight movement, turnover, top-holding flips, risk drift under the unperturbed cloud, and realized next-quarter outcome of the perturbed versus base weights, in-sample.

Side-check. Propagate the belief error into the scenario cloud location too and re-solve, to test whether holding scenarios fixed is justified.

Outcome

Six findings, from the report.

  1. High weight sensitivity, low realized sensitivity. A one-unit error moves a large share of the book and flips a top holding in most draws, yet CVaR stays pinned at the budget (every perturbed point sits on it) and the median realized outcome cost is essentially zero with a wide interval. Expected-return error is uncompensated noise in composition, not realized loss, and it cannot degrade tail risk.
  2. Signal equals error. The plausible error unit is a multiple of the cross-sectional spread of the expected returns themselves, and a one-unit perturbation moves weights about as far as production expected returns move them from the pure-risk portfolio. The cross-section is trusted at the edge of its own noise floor.
  3. Ranks matter as much as levels. The adjacent-rank scramble, the gentlest ordering error, is among the most damaging.
  4. The bond yield-anchor sleeve is the single highest-leverage input, and the common level shift is strongly asymmetric because the bond sleeve is anchored rather than shifted, so lowering equity assumptions stampedes the book into bonds. It is also the one part of the layer that is testable at the level.
  5. Shrinkage is a mild lever, protecting against independent error but not factor-aligned error.
  6. Robustness is counter-cyclical: error matters most in calm and least in stress, because the budget binds hardest in high volatility and forces the robust leftmost point.

Side-check, reported honestly against the prereg’s expectation. The cloud channel is not negligible at the largest scale, so the headline sensitivities are a lower bound.

Decision and the aim statement for the mu program

Nothing changed. The report set the program’s order: first, the make-or-break question of whether the equity and alternatives cross-section beats equal expected returns out of sample, net of its own error (rank-IC study); second, pin the bond yield-anchor sleeve hard (bond-sleeve level study); deprioritize the common equity-premium level, which the budget already neutralizes. Urgency was rated moderate, not extreme: the risk posture is firewalled. The finding that cross-section quality is immaterial to outcomes was recorded as conditional on the current duration-heavy, risk-firewalled posture, the seed of the binding conditionality clause that now sits in the spec.

Where it led

The whole mu program followed the order set here, through the simplification and the later reverse-optimization adoption, whose gates reuse this study’s frozen evaluation panel for risk non-degradation.

NoteOwn verdict and decision of record

The rank-IC report’s own verdict is “CROSS-SECTION VALIDATES” (mechanical, prereg-bound), scored on the causal reconstruction at the pre-registered primary cell. The decision of record puts the within-sleeve “CMA cross-section” on the rejected list, retired by the simplification, and keeps the between-sleeve gap as “the one validated rank content”. The model specification gives the reason: the validated content of the ranking “is sleeve-level only; within-sleeve is indistinguishable-to-inverted under causal loadings”. The frozen-loadings variant was “reported, never verdict-driving”; the sign reversal between frozen and causal loadings is in the equities-only ordering, not in the verdict.

Rank-IC study: does the expected-return cross-section carry real ordering information? (validates mechanically; the content is sleeve-level only, and the equity ordering inverts under causal loadings)

Logged 2026-07-23.

A pre-registered, causally reconstructed test of whether production expected returns’ asset ordering contains out-of-sample information net of its own error. The mechanical verdict at the primary horizon passed, but attribution showed the validated content is the between-sleeve split only; within-sleeve ordering is indistinguishable-to-inverted, the frozen-loadings look-ahead leak was quantified, and two standing methodology rules came out of it.

Question (registered verbatim)

Does production expected returns’ asset ordering contain real out-of-sample information, net of its own error, versus equal-expected-return and zero-expected-return baselines and a naive momentum rank? Motivated by the sensitivity study: the plausible error is a multiple of the cross-sectional spread and optimizer sensitivity is rank-dominated.

Pre-registration (frozen before any outcome scoring)

Construction under test. The production layer from the expected-returns build: factor projection plus the keep-fraction of implied alpha for the risky sleeve, the starting-yield override for the bond sleeve.

Causal reconstruction. Production expected returns reproduced at each weekly start using only information available then: point-in-time yields, the equity-premium vintages of each prior year, and annual causal expanding-window refits of the loadings, over the sub-universe that can be reconstructed point-in-time. The reproduction was checked against the frozen production vector. Variants: the frozen production loadings (non-causal, longer panel, kept to measure the leak), no bond override, the raw point-in-time consensus, pure projection with zero keep-fraction, and a trailing-return momentum rank.

Scoring and nulls. Spearman rank IC against forward realized rankings at three horizons with the middle one primary; top-minus-bottom spreads; paired comparison against momentum. The primary null band comes from persistence-preserving static random rankings, because the signal is slow-moving and evaluation windows overlap; the literal per-date permutation band is reported only as the overlap-naive contrast. Attribution splits the IC into between-sleeve and within-sleeve (demeaned) components, and an equities-only read. Minimum detectable IC reported first.

Outcome

Result: mechanical verdict CROSS-SECTION VALIDATES at the primary horizon; attribution overturns what that means.

  • The mean IC cleared the persistence-preserving null band, the top-minus-bottom spread was positive with a confidence interval clear of zero, and the ordering did not lose to momentum. The registered verdict fired.
  • The validated content is entirely the between-sleeve ordering: risk assets above defensives, plus bond-duration ordering. Within sleeves, the demeaned IC sits inside its null band; the equities-only IC is inverted and confidence-interval negative under causal loadings; the ordering is statistically indistinguishable from a trailing momentum rank; and the sleeve call itself inverts for the pre-2010 starts.
  • The minimum detectable IC at this sample exceeds any plausible anchor IC, so the within-sleeve question is unanswerable here.
  • The frozen-loadings look-ahead leak, quantified. On identical dates the equity IC under the frozen production loadings is positive while the causal-loadings IC is negative. Any expected-return backtest through frozen loadings inherits this optimism. The build’s Test 1 is reinterpreted as the keep-fraction selection curve, not validation.

Decision

Production expected returns stood unchanged. Two standing rules, named and binding on every future expected-return evaluation, came out of this study and the sleeve study that followed: causal inputs everywhere (reconstruct every input using only prior data, and flag any input that cannot be; nothing frozen from the future may touch an evaluation), and persistence-preserving nulls (static random rankings for cross-sections, circular block shifts for levels, never per-date permutation, with the effective sample and minimum detectable effect reported first). The recorded implication, not executed and requiring its own prereg: a sleeve-level simplification (yield-anchored bonds plus one equity-premium block) or the expected-return-invariant risk-parity path, pending the bond-sleeve level study. The within-sleeve cross-section later went on the rejected list.

Where it led

The bond-sleeve level study tested the level half this attribution left untested, and the simplification then retired the within-sleeve cross-section. The full-model assessment later found the deployed ordering’s rank IC inside the persistence null band at both horizons, as this record predicted.

Bond-sleeve level study: does the yield anchor predict sleeve returns at the level? (SLEEVE ANCHOR VALIDATES; the machinery is the raw yield)

Logged 2026-07-23.

A pre-registered, causally reconstructed test of the expected-return input the duration-heavy book actually leans on. The anchor cleared the persistence-preserving null at the level and beat trailing returns decisively; beyond the raw observable curve yield the machinery adds nothing, and the horizon mismatch entering the 2022 hiking cycle is carried as a known risk rather than a defect.

Question (registered)

Does the production yield-anchored bond-sleeve expected return predict realized sleeve returns at its anchor horizon, and does its machinery add anything beyond the raw observable yield? This is the input the current allocation leans on: the frozen production walk-forward held most of the book in the bond sleeve entering 2021 to 2022, and the sensitivity study flagged the sleeve anchor as the single highest-leverage expected-return input. The rank-IC study validated only the sleeve-level ordering; the level itself is what this study tests.

Pre-registration (frozen before any outcome scoring)

Construction under test. The production per-ticker anchor: the nominal curve interpolated to each fund’s static duration, an inflation-linked fund as a nominal equivalent, investment grade as curve plus a long-history corporate spread, minus per-ticker credit haircuts. The high-yield and emerging-market names use effective-yield series that exist only from 2023 onward, so they are historically unevaluable and were excluded, flagged.

Causal reconstruction. Every input strictly through the prior week, production code verbatim, at every weekly start from early 2008 over the six-name point-in-time sub-sleeve. Primary object: the equal-weight sleeve expected return versus the realized forward sleeve return at the nearest feasible horizon to the anchor’s ten-year construction, with a shorter horizon reported and a longer one descriptive.

Baselines and null. Raw curve yield, a naive flat long-rate, trailing realized return, and zero; a circular block-shift persistence-preserving null; effective sample size and minimum detectable effect reported first, per the standing rules carried verbatim in the prereg.

Outcome

Result: SLEEVE ANCHOR VALIDATES. Validated content: the raw observable yield. Raw yield suffices for the machinery.

  • Power first: the effective sample is a couple of epochs, and the null band is wide. The anchor’s correlation with forward realized sleeve return clears the null’s upper quantile by a wide margin; the mean level error is unbiased with the interval containing zero.
  • The slope at the primary horizon is far above one, a duration-mechanical amplification that collapses toward identity at the anchor’s own ten-year horizon, the causal reproduction of the build’s Test 2.
  • It beats trailing return decisively on both correlation and error.
  • Machinery earns nothing. Beyond the raw curve yield the adjustments reduce to a constant of a few basis points, and the duration and spread time-variation points negative against the naive flat rate, below its own null band. A simplification may anchor to the raw observable yield with no measured loss; the untested high-yield and emerging-market haircuts are an extrapolation zone, retained on reasoning.
  • The 2022 exhibit, on record. Entering the hiking cycle the anchor overstated sleeve returns at every window, worst at the short horizon and shrinking at the primary one, with a material book-level drag at the worst window given the sleeve weight. The trailing-return alternative was several times worse at the same dates. This horizon mismatch is a structural limitation the book carries as risk, not a fixable defect.

Decision

Production expected returns stood unchanged. Two spec additions: the named standing rules (causal inputs everywhere; persistence-preserving nulls with power reported first), and the binding conditionality clause, verbatim: “The finding that mu cross-section quality is immaterial to outcomes is CONDITIONAL on the current duration-heavy, risk-firewalled posture. If risk budgets rise, leverage is added, or BL views push weight into risky sleeves, the equity cross-section question MUST be reopened under a new prereg before those weights ship.” Recorded implication: the sleeve-level-only simplification prereg is now fully evidence-backed and may proceed.

Where it led

The simplification adopted the raw curve yield as the defensive-sleeve expected return the same day. The full-model assessment found the pooled sleeve levels calibrate at both horizons and the 2021 to 2023 rate-shock miss reproduces.

Mu simplification adopted: production expected returns become sleeve-level (all gates pass; the consensus cross-section retired to rollback)

Logged 2026-07-23.

A gated adoption study, not open research: the licensing verdicts were complete. The defensive sleeve became the raw observable curve yield at the as-of date and the risky sleeve a single frozen equity-premium block. Every gate passed, including a risk non-degradation gate on the frozen sensitivity panel, and the composition shift it caused was measured as a label shift, not a risk shift.

What this was

Step two of the recorded expected-returns sequence, executed as a gated adoption rather than open research. The licensing verdicts were complete: the sensitivity study (composition-fragile, risk-firewalled, realized cost near zero), the rank-IC study (only the sleeve-level ordering validates; the equity ordering inverts under causal loadings), and the bond-sleeve level study (the anchor validates and the raw yield suffices).

Pre-registration (every gate band a pre-registered number, frozen before any scoring)

The change. The defensive sleeve’s expected return becomes each ticker’s raw observable curve yield at the as-of date, the production yield code verbatim, with the tested haircuts dropped for the point-in-time-evaluable names and the untested high-yield and emerging-market haircuts retained as-is (a deliberate asymmetry: tested adjustments retired, untested ones kept on reasoning, disclosed). The risky sleeve’s expected return becomes one frozen equity-premium block at the frozen production equity-core level, uniform across the sleeve, the rank-IC verdict that only the sleeve level validated. No new estimated parameters. The vector serves as both the optimizer objective and the scenario location; the certified covariance, the dial, the generator, and the optimizer are untouched. The old cross-section path is preserved bit-for-bit as a one-line rollback.

Gates, all hard except the descriptive one. G1: reproduction of the sleeve study’s raw yield series and the exact risky block across every grid date. G2: risk non-degradation on the frozen sensitivity-study evaluation panel, with worst-case CVaR and volatility changes inside pre-registered bands. G3: a composition split alarm against a fresh-seed sensitivity band, frozen before comparison, with the prediction written down first. G4: an outcome sanity read against the recorded near-zero prediction, descriptive. G5: a wiring smoke test, flag-off bit-identical to the captured pre-change baseline and the certified covariance, dial, and degrees of freedom untouched flag-on. Any failure stops the study and leaves the old mode in place.

Outcome

Result: ALL GATES PASS.

  • Reproduction was exact across the grid; risk non-degradation held within its bands; the wiring smoke test was bit-identical.
  • The honest read on the composition gate, on record. Composition moved more than predicted: the median book move was large, most weeks changed a top-three holding, and the median risky share roughly doubled, because the optimizer harvests a uniform premium through the low-volatility risky names. Tail risk stayed pinned at the CVaR budget. A label shift, not a risk shift, which is exactly what the risk gate exists to catch, and the split alarm stayed clear of its band.

Decision

The sleeve-level mode was defaulted on, with the cross-section path as the rollback. The walk-forward confirmation script was extended to score the new and old expected returns on post-freeze weeks, refusing to score unless the gate record is a pass. The live-allocation builder branches on the mode, and the executed live notebook was deliberately not regenerated: the change reaches the book on the standing re-estimation and drift clocks, at a priced one-off turnover. Expected returns joined the model of record with every component evidence-backed or explicitly characterized, the horizon mismatch documented as a structural limitation, and the binding conditionality clause cross-referenced. On the rejected list: the within-sleeve consensus cross-section, the duration and spread and haircut adjustments on the tested names, and the keep-fraction, retired with the cross-section rather than condemned.

Where it led

The uniform block’s mechanical flaw, a constant numerator harvested through low-risk names, became the question of the next three studies. Two history-estimated cross-sections failed their auditions (factor-implied, observable anchors) and a covariance-sourced shape passed (reverse optimization), leading to the reverse-optimization adoption that now sits on top of this sleeve-level base. See the Current Model page.

Factor-implied expected returns: does pricing the risky sleeve through factor exposures beat the uniform block? (UNIFORM BLOCK STANDS)

Logged 2026-07-24.

The first richer expected-return candidate under the program’s third step, and the governed test of the spec’s warning that historical factor means are the sample-mean error-maximizer in factor costume. The diversifier exhibit was fully adjudicable and factor pricing lost it on content; out-of-era premia inverted the ordering and degraded realized tails.

Question (registered)

Does pricing the risky-sleeve cross-section through factor exposures, each asset’s causal loadings times historical factor premia, add validated structure beyond the deployed uniform equity-premium block? In particular, does it correctly discount the low-beta diversifiers (minimum-volatility equity, managed futures, bank loans, the dollar) that the uniform block credits at the full premium and that the optimizer now loads after the simplification? Research and characterization only, engine read-only; any adoption would route through the conditionality clause.

Pre-registration (frozen before any scoring)

Candidates. The incumbent uniform block; factor-implied expected returns with causal annual production-estimator loadings and expanding-mean historical factor premia on the observable core, latent components excluded as unpriced; the premia shrunk toward market-only at several strengths; and the premia frozen on pre-2010 data only, the crisis-dominated era. The bond sleeve identical across all candidates. The wiring gate reproduced the simplification’s frozen optimizer chain on every panel week exactly before anything was scored.

Verdict logic, three-way, with power first. The ordering minimum detectable effect over the reconstructable universe reported first; if ordering is out of reach, the verdict routes through the pre-registered diversifier level exhibit (does the candidate price the four named diversifiers nearer their realized returns than the block, with a minimum count of wins) plus a realized-tail gate on the resulting allocations. The spec’s prior on record, that historical factor means are the warned-against construction, was tested here under governance rather than assumed.

Outcome

Result: UNIFORM BLOCK STANDS.

  • Power first. The ordering minimum detectable effect at the primary horizon was out of reach, so the verdict routed through the level exhibit as registered.
