This program asked whether the model portfolio should carry position-level protection against crash arrival, the entry weeks of a correlation-structure break that the certified risk model cannot cover, and how much return such protection would give up. A pre-registered study, frozen and hashed before scoring, characterized always-on and strictly causal sizing and overlay policies; by its own rules it could name no winner and give no adoption verdict. Crash-arrival risk and the sizing/overlay frontier (characterized; adoption open; do-nothing stands)

Logged 2026-07-22.

The one blind spot the distribution stack provably cannot cover is crash arrival: a correlation-structure break at entry, unreachable by any strictly causal signal before the entry week. A pre-registered characterization of position-level sizing and overlay policies against it, ending in a decision menu with no recommendation.

The blind spot

The certified production risk model has exactly one proven structural blind spot: the entry weeks of a correlation-structure break, crash arrival. The 2020 arrival was not a large market draw pulled through stable betas; the co-movement structure re-wired inside the week. A trailing estimator cannot see it coming by construction, and the V3 dial is a persistence bet that lags entries. The chain of studies proving that no distribution machinery covers it is on the record: every scenario variant in the scenario-layer audit, including an explicit jump overlay, pins the crash-core weeks beyond its cloud; the rebase left the arrival exhibit unchanged by pre-registered hard check; and the deployed single-regime generator carries the same pins. Conclusion of record: protection against arrival, if bought at all, is bought at the position level, by sizing or overlay, not from distribution machinery.

Pre-registration (frozen and hashed before scoring; characterization only)

No verdict permitted. The prereg states that this is a characterization: no winner, no adoption verdict. The deliverable is the measured frontier plus a decision menu. A provisional-cost caveat was carried verbatim: the baseline CVaR numbers came from the scenario layer known to be mis-wired at the time, so every cost calibration was provisional pending the rebase, while the arrival-severity facts are rebase-invariant because a modest cloud widening does not reach a multiple. An honesty block: one arrival episode dominates the protection axis; the other stress episodes provide persistence context only; every frontier conclusion is conditional on that single episode and must say so.

Setup. The frozen weekly grid; the do-nothing baseline is the production causal weights as of the prior week. Sixteen configurations in four policy families: always-on gross reduction at several levels; a volatility-conditioned de-risking on the dial’s own z-score, the negative control for conditioning; fast-signal de-risking on five-day realized market volatility or a prior-week drawdown trigger; and a tail-budget sizing rule that scales gross so predicted CVaR times an arrival multiplier stays within a weekly loss budget. Every policy is either always-on or conditions only on strictly causal signals, asserted per week. The de-risked fraction earns zero, so every drag figure is an upper bound. Paired stationary bootstrap intervals on every frontier point.

Outcome

Findings of record (no verdict).

  • The fire-date table is the key exhibit. Before the first arrival week the market was flat to calm through the Friday close: the dial’s z-score was negative, five-day realized volatility was low, the prior week was positive. No causal signal in any pre-registered family fires before week one. The entry week is unprotectable by conditioning of any speed; only always-on gross reduction is smaller when it hits. Weeks two and three are reachable by the fast signals and, in this episode, even by the dial’s own z-score, because the crash week enters the trailing window immediately.
  • The frontier is axis-dependent; there is no single frontier. On full-period realized CVaR the always-on line dominates nearly every conditional configuration, because unconditional scaling trims every tail week including the signal-less entries. On the worst-week and arrival axes the ranking inverts: fired conditioners de-risk harder than any tolerable always-on point. The cheap configurations that sit above the always-on line earn their edge almost entirely inside the 2020 episode.
  • The volatility-conditioned negative control confirmed the lag exactly and only where it matters most, the entry week, while delivering the persistence-episode protection it was designed for in 2022.
  • The tail-budget family spans the extremes and is the rebase-sensitive one, because its sizing paths consume the predicted CVaR series.

Decision

A five-row decision menu is on record: do nothing; an always-on gross level; de-risk on the dial’s z-score; de-risk on the fast realized-volatility signal; and the tail-budget rule (provisional pending re-scoring). No recommendation, no adoption. Adoption of any row requires a follow-up prereg that re-scores only the selected row plus the do-nothing baseline against the rebased CVaR, same grid, metrics, and seeds, with no second selection pass over the grid, before any engine change. Do nothing stands while undecided. The rebase has since landed and re-anchored the arrival severities (conclusion unchanged); the tail-budget numbers remain rebase-sensitive until the adoption prereg re-scores them.

Where it led

The arrival blind spot is recorded as a known limitation of the model of record and as an open decision on the Current Model page. The full-model assessment found the allocation’s CVaR breach depth concentrated exactly at this blind spot, and the mean-reversion study routed a short-horizon crash-rebound shape here rather than into expected returns.
